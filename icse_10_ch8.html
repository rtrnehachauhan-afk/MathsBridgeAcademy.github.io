📄1. What is a Geometric Progression?▾
GEOMETRIC PROGRESSION (G.P.)
A sequence of numbers in which every term (after the first) is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a fixed, non-zero number. This fixed multiplier is called the common ratio, written r. If the first term is a, the G.P. looks like a, ar, ar², ar³, …
SymbolMeaning
aFirst term of the G.P.
rCommon ratio = (any term) ÷ (the term just before it)
nNumber of terms / the position of a term
tₙ (or aₙ)The nth term (general term)
lLast term of the G.P.
⚠️ MBA Note: To check if a sequence is a G.P., divide EVERY consecutive pair — one matching ratio isn't enough, all of them must give the same r. Never subtract to check a G.P. — that tests for an A.P. instead
🧮2. The nth Term (General Term)▾
nth term formula
\( t_n = a \cdot r^{n-1} \)
Gives the value of the term sitting at position n, without listing every term before it.
nth TERM FROM THE END — REVERSE-GP LOGIC
Reading a G.P. backward gives another G.P.: its first term is the original last term (l), and its common ratio is 1/r. So the "nth term from the end" is nothing new — it is just the ordinary nth-term formula applied to this reversed G.P.
Step 1 — form the reversed G.P.
\( a' = l, \qquad r' = \dfrac{1}{r} \)
Step 2 — apply the usual nth-term formula to it
\( t_n = a'\cdot(r')^{n-1} = \dfrac{l}{r^{n-1}} \)
The compact form l/rⁿ⁻¹ is just this same working with a' and r' substituted back in — derive it fresh each time rather than quoting it as a separate rule to memorise.
WORKING BACKWARDS — "WHICH TERM IS X?"
Set tₙ equal to the given value and solve the same formula for n instead of for the term. Since n appears as an exponent, this usually means writing both sides as powers of the same base and equating the exponents.
FINDING a AND r FROM TWO GIVEN TERMS
Write tₙ = a·rⁿ⁻¹ for each given term — this gives two equations in a and r. Divide one equation by the other so a cancels, leaving an equation purely in r.
Σ3. Sum of n Terms (Sₙ)▾
Sum formula — when r > 1
\( S_n = \dfrac{a(r^n-1)}{r-1} \)
Sum formula — when r < 1
\( S_n = \dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r} \)
Both formulas give the same value for a given G.P. — the two versions just avoid dividing by a negative number, keeping the working tidy.
⚠️ MBA Note: Series like 9+99+999+… or 7+77+777+… are NOT themselves a G.P. — rewrite each term as (10ᵏ − 1)×constant first, split into a G.P. part and a "−n" part, then sum each separately
🎯4. The Smart Substitution Trick▾
WHY IT WORKS
When a question gives the SUM or PRODUCT of a small, specific number of terms in G.P. (and asks you to find the actual numbers), don't use a, ar, ar², … Instead centre the terms symmetrically around the middle so the ratio cancels out of the product completely.

Use the SECOND piece of given information (sum, difference, a ratio, etc.) to form an equation in r and solve for it. Then convert the symmetric terms (a/r, a, ar, …) back into the actual numbers of the sequence.
⚠️ MBA Note: Insert-k-geometric-means problems (e.g. "insert 5 terms between 4374 and 6") are really a "find r" nth-term problem in disguise — the two given numbers are just the first and last term of a longer G.P.
🗂️5. The 7 Question Types — Spot the Pattern First▾
WHY THIS MATTERS
Every question in this chapter is really one of only 7 recurring patterns. Before you start solving, ask "which type is this?" — once you recognise the type, you already know the shape of the solution, even before you've done any working.
